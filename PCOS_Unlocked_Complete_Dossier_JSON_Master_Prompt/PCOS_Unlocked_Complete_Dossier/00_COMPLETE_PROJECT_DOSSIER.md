## 1. Project summary

PCOS Unlocked is an educational website designed to make information about Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) accessible, understandable and engaging for people across age groups. It has a shared learning foundation and two distinct pathways:
- **Female learning pathway:** body literacy, PCOS features, menstrual and reproductive health, diagnosis, management and wellbeing.
- **Male ally pathway:** understanding PCOS, challenging misconceptions, understanding possible impacts and learning how to support someone respectfully.

The pathways are not access restrictions. All visitors can explore all shared content, change pathways at any time and skip onboarding.

**Core proposition:** A calm, illustration-led learning environment that explains complex health information through clear diagrams, interactive learning and trustworthy references.

## 2. Design problem and opportunity

PCOS information can be difficult to navigate because medical terminology, myths, stigma, variable symptoms and fragmented information make it hard for people to understand the condition. People around someone with PCOS may also lack basic knowledge and may not know how to offer respectful support.

**Design opportunity:** translate complex, evidence-informed information into a clear, age-aware, interactive learning experience that supports body literacy, informed questions and empathy without attempting diagnosis.

## 3. Aim and objectives

### Aim
To develop an inclusive digital learning platform that increases understanding of PCOS and supports informed, non-judgemental conversations about reproductive, hormonal and metabolic health.

### Objectives
1. Explain body anatomy, menstrual health, hormones and metabolism using accessible visual communication.
2. Explain PCOS, its varied features, clinical assessment and management in plain language.
3. Adapt language and depth for different age ranges without blocking access to information.
4. Provide distinct female and male ally learning journeys.
5. Challenge common misconceptions with sourced explanations.
6. Help visitors recognise when professional advice may be appropriate.
7. Provide a smooth multi-page experience with accessible navigation.
8. Make all health claims traceable to credible sources.

## 4. Audience

### Female learning pathway
For people seeking to understand PCOS and related body/health topics. Do not assume every visitor menstruates, is sexually active, wants pregnancy, has a diagnosis or has the same symptoms.

### Male ally pathway
For boys and men who want to understand PCOS and support a friend, partner, sibling or family member. Do not frame males as PCOS patients or create a male PCOS diagnosis flow.

### Other audiences
Parents/carers, educators and anyone wanting reliable introductory information.

## 13. Page content requirements

### Homepage
Headline: **Your body deserves clear answers.**  
Subhead: **Explore PCOS, hormones and reproductive health through clear explanations, interactive visuals and myth-busting guides.**  
Primary CTA: Explore PCOS. Secondary CTA: Choose your learning route.  
Show Know Your Body, Understand PCOS, Female pathway, Male ally pathway and Find Support.

### Know Your Body
Explain that body literacy supports understanding of health information. Link anatomy, menstrual cycle, hormones and metabolism.

### Reproductive anatomy
Label ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix and vagina. State diagram is simplified and not to scale. Include text alternative.

### Menstrual cycle
Explain menstruation, follicular phase, ovulation and luteal phase. Emphasise cycle length and timing vary; 28 days is not universal and ovulation does not occur in every cycle.

### Hormones
Explain hormones as chemical messengers. Introduce GnRH, FSH, LH, oestrogen, progesterone, androgens and insulin at an appropriate level. Avoid implying one hormone alone explains PCOS.

### Insulin and metabolism
Explain glucose and insulin. Insulin resistance can be associated with PCOS for some people but is not universal and cannot be identified by appearance. Do not claim weight loss cures PCOS.

### What is PCOS?
Explain PCOS as a complex hormonal and metabolic condition with variable presentations. The name can be misleading: ovarian appearance alone does not define PCOS.

### Features and experiences
Possible features include irregular/absent periods, unpredictable ovulation, acne, excess facial/body hair, scalp hair thinning and metabolic features in some people. Symptoms overlap with other conditions; no checklist can diagnose.

### Diagnosis
Explain that diagnosis requires qualified clinical assessment and exclusion of other causes. Adult diagnostic guidance generally considers two of three features: ovulatory dysfunction, clinical/biochemical hyperandrogenism, and polycystic ovarian morphology (with AMH as an adult alternative in guideline contexts). If irregular cycles and hyperandrogenism are both present, ultrasound/AMH may not be required. Adolescents require both ovulatory dysfunction and hyperandrogenism; ultrasound/AMH are not recommended for adolescent diagnosis. Clinical content must be checked against the current guideline and reviewed by a clinician before launch.

### Health associations
Explain possible associations with metabolic health, sleep-related issues, psychological distress and reproductive challenges. Individual risks vary. Do not use deterministic language.

### Management
Explain that care is individualised and may include cycle regulation, symptom management, metabolic risk management, fertility support if desired and psychological support. Medication decisions belong to clinicians. No universal diet or cure.

### Female pathway
Focus on lived experiences, menstrual/reproductive health, diagnosis, care conversations, body image and mental wellbeing. Do not assume fertility goals or sexual activity.

### Male ally pathway
Explain the condition, correct myths, address stigma and show how to listen, ask what support is wanted, respect privacy and avoid unsolicited advice.

### Myths and facts
Include:
- PCOS is only an ovarian problem — incomplete/false.
- Everyone with PCOS has ovarian cysts — false.
- Only people with larger bodies have PCOS — false.
- PCOS means someone cannot become pregnant — false.
- Lifestyle alone cures PCOS — false.
- Pads, tampons or menstrual cups cause PCOS — no established evidence supports this claim.
- An online quiz can diagnose PCOS — false.

Each answer must have a short explanation and source.

### Quiz
5–8 educational questions; show explanation after each answer. No diagnosis, risk score or treatment recommendation.

### Find Support
Encourage professional advice for persistent irregular/absent periods, distressing symptoms, new/rapidly worsening symptoms, fertility concerns if relevant, or metabolic/mental health concerns. Avoid alarmist language. For urgent/severe symptoms, advise local urgent/emergency care.

### Appointment preparation
Prompts: What changes have I noticed? When did they begin? How have cycles changed, if relevant? Which symptoms affect daily life? What tests or other causes may need consideration? What are benefits, risks and alternatives of proposed care? What follow-up is recommended?

## 17. Medical safety and terminology

**Disclaimer:** “PCOS Unlocked is an educational resource. It does not provide a diagnosis or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.”

Use PCOS as the main public/search term. If emerging terminology such as PMOS is discussed, clearly label it as an evolving terminology discussion, cite the exact source and date, and do not silently replace PCOS throughout the site.

Do not:
- diagnose from symptoms, images, quiz answers, BMI or lab values;
- promise a cure or recommend medication doses;
- imply all people with PCOS have ovarian cysts;
- claim weight, diet, laziness, stress or menstrual products cause PCOS;
- make fertility deterministic;
- confuse association with causation;
- present a 28-day cycle as universal;
- publish unverified dataset percentages.

Every medical page needs references and a review date. A qualified healthcare professional should review the content before public launch. Never pretend such review has happened if it has not.

## 18. Dataset audit and limitations

The supplied files were inspected by schema:
- `pcos_dataset.csv`: 1,000 rows × 6 columns — Age, BMI, Menstrual_Irregularity, Testosterone_Level(ng/dL), Antral_Follicle_Count, PCOS_Diagnosis.
- `Cleaned-Data.csv`: 173 rows × 36 columns — includes age, weight/height, PCOS and family history fields, menstrual/hormonal features, mental health, conception difficulty, insulin resistance, diabetes, diet-frequency variables, exercise, sleep, stress, smoking and medication.

These are not the separate male/female awareness Google Forms described in the project history. Neither schema contains a gender field or direct awareness questions. They cannot support male-versus-female awareness comparisons.

The provenance, sampling, population, diagnostic definitions and representativeness of the CSVs are not established by the files alone. Some fields in the 173-row file are self-reported, compound or free text. Do not treat them as verified diagnoses or standardised treatment categories without a documented data dictionary and cleaning method.

Permitted use: cautious exploratory description of the supplied sample after checking coding and missingness.  
Not permitted: Indian population prevalence estimates, causal claims, diagnostic use, or gender-awareness comparisons.
